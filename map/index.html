Methodology & sources

How this was built

Where the data comes from

Every figure here comes from the U.S. EPA Safe Drinking Water Information System (SDWIS), the federal database of record for public drinking water compliance, populated by state agencies (MassDEP, CT DPH, RI DOH, VT DEC, ME DWP, NH DES) from the results every regulated system reports.

  • Map bands and summary figures: ECHO SDWA quarterly download, 2026 Q2 refresh
  • Year-by-year detail: EPA Envirofacts REST service — VIOLATION, LCR_SAMPLE, LCR_SAMPLE_RESULT, pulled for all six states and checked against EPA's own row counts
  • Contaminant names: EPA SDWA_REF_CODE_VALUES (871 official codes) — not our own labels
  • Town coordinates: U.S. Census 2022 Gazetteer

Dates are monitoring periods, not sample days

This matters for reading every date here. The dates attached to these records are the boundaries of the compliance monitoring period a result is filed against — not the day someone drew water from a tap.

  • Lead and copper results carry the period start and end. A sample was collected somewhere inside that window; the record does not say when.
  • Violations carry the compliance period they belong to, which is why a single ongoing problem can appear as one entry per quarter.

What "no violations recorded" means

It means nothing was filed against that town's systems in the window — that they met their reporting requirements. It does not mean nothing is present in the water, and it does not mean everything was tested for. A system reports on the contaminants it is required to monitor. Anything outside that list does not appear here because nobody measured it.

The zeros

Of 24,100 lead results across New England, 7,390 are recorded as exactly zero. A laboratory does not measure zero — it measures down to a limit and stops. A zero here means nothing was found above the point the instrument could still detect, which is a smaller statement than none.

How the band is calculated

Health-based violations (a contaminant above a federal limit, or a treatment failure) are weighted three times more heavily than monitoring and reporting violations (testing or filing late).

raw = (health_violations × 3) + monitoring_violations
intensity = raw / (population_served / 100)
index = min(100, ln(1 + raw) × 15 + ln(1 + intensity) × 10)

The log scale stops one large utility dominating. A town's band is then raised if any single measurement is severe, so a town with one very high lead reading is flagged even with no violations on paper.

This index describes a compliance record, not a health risk. It measures what a system reported to its regulator. It is not a measure of how safe anyone's water is, and it says nothing about any individual house.

What is excluded

  • Private wells. Roughly one New England household in five. No federal reporting obligation exists, so no record exists.
  • Everything never tested for. Not the same as absent.
  • 56 of 1,207 aggregated town rows could not be matched to a Census coordinate and are not mapped. Almost entirely very small systems.

What this cannot tell you

Everything here was measured in a water system, at compliance sample points, on particular dates. None of it was measured at any individual tap. Between the treatment plant and a kitchen faucet there is a distribution network, a service line whose material the utility may not know, the building's own plumbing, and the fixture itself. Lead in particular is almost never in the source water — it is picked up on the way. A public record describes the system. Only a sample from a tap describes that tap.
